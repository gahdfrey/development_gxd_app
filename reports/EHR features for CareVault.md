# Build HMO claims and safety rails first

CareVault's biggest gap is commercial. Nigerian competitors and regional national EHRs now compete on insurance revenue: HMO and NHIA tariffs, authorization codes, claim batches and reconciliation. CareVault has payments and wallets but no payer-aware billing ledger. The second gap is clinical. CareVault prescribes by generic but has no structured allergy list, problem list, vitals flowsheet or eMAR. Those four records are the base for the features with the strongest evidence of reducing harm: medication decision support, which roughly halves medication errors; eMAR, which essentially eliminates transcription errors; and early-warning scores. The third gap is regulatory, and it is now concrete. Nigeria has a draft national FHIR R4 implementation guide (Nigeria Core). It profiles exactly the resources CareVault already stores, so building to it now costs little and is unlikely to be wasted. GAID 2025 also adds DPIA, breach and annual audit duties that a SaaS EHR can turn into product features. This report recommends **17 P0 features**, most of them small or medium effort, and a longer P1/P2 backlog grouped into eight areas. Effort ratings are my own estimates for this codebase: **S** is under about 2 engineer-weeks, **M** is about 2–6, and **L** is more than 6. Following the product owner's decision, the report does not recommend reintroducing 2FA or idle auto-logoff as defaults.

## Insurance revenue is the gap Nigerian competitors already fill

The clearest regional trend for 2025–2026 is that EHRs are becoming the route through which insurance claims are submitted. From July 2026, Kenya's SHA requires all Level 4 public hospitals to submit new claims only through the Taifa Care HMIS ([The Star](https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/2026-07-01-sha-shifts-level-4-public-hospitals-to-taifa-care-hmis)). Ghana's LHIMS sites credit the system with gains in revenue management ([Citi Newsroom](https://citinewsroom.com/2025/02/how-ghanas-lightwave-health-information-management-system-is-transforming-healthcare-delivery/)). In Nigeria, HeliumOS advertises tariff and scheme management, claims generation and tracking, and charges consolidated automatically at discharge ([HeliumOS](https://heliumhealth.com/helium-os/)). Hyella sells HMO reconciliation, capitation agreements and handling of patients who switch from private pay to HMO cover ([Hyella](https://hyella.com/hmo-reconciliation/)).

Nigerian HMO billing is still mostly manual. Hospitals verify the enrollee, obtain an authorization code "via WhatsApp or phone call", and send claims that are rejected if the code is missing ([Pharmacare](https://pharmacare.app/blog/hmo-billing-dental-clinic-nigeria)). Payment typically arrives 30–90+ days later, and common rejection reasons are expired authorization, unverified eligibility, coding mismatches and late submission ([AjirMed](https://ajirmed.com/step-by-step-guide-to-registering-your-hospital-in-nigeria-for-hmo-and-getting-paid-faster.electronic-medical-records-emr-nigeria)). Both of these are vendor blogs. Regulation is tightening, which makes an evidence trail valuable. NHIA reportedly requires HMOs to answer pre-authorization requests within **one hour** from April 2025, with escalation to NHIA state offices and penalties for HMOs that miss the deadline. That report comes from a secondary source; the circular itself was not found ([Streamlinefeed](https://streamlinefeed.co.ke/news/nhia-enforces-one-hour-hmo-authorisation-policy-to-end-hospital-delays)). NHIA also raised fee-for-service rates by **378%** and capitation by **93%**, effective April 2025 ([Guardian NG](https://guardian.ng/news/nhia-increases-capitation-service-fees/)). A timestamped authorization log with an SLA timer therefore protects revenue and documents the hospital's case if it needs to escalate to NHIA. That is a feature few local vendors advertise.

Big-vendor RCM rests on four patterns that work at CareVault's scale. Charges route automatically from clinical events, and bad charges are held in an error pool ([UC Davis audit of Epic](https://auditreports.ucop.edu/?action=public_ar_display&id=ec1a0b2003913b76)). Claims are checked by pre-submission edits. Denials and other exceptions go to work queues ([Mindbowser](https://www.mindbowser.com/epic-resolute-denials-management/)). Clinical systems are reconciled against billing. The tax and payment layer also needs care. Under the Nigeria Tax Act 2025, medical services, pharmaceuticals and medical equipment are **zero-rated, not exempt** ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/tax-authorities/1793598/examination-of-the-value-added-tax-provisions-under-the-nigeria-tax-act-2025)), so each catalog item needs its own tax code. NRS e-invoicing is being phased in by taxpayer size, though sources disagree on the dates for medium and small taxpayers ([Sovos](https://sovos.com/regulatory-updates/vat/nigeria-nrs-publishes-e-invoicing-timeline/); [VATupdate](https://www.vatupdate.com/2026/02/19/briefing-document-podcast-e-invoicing-and-e-reporting-in-nigeria/)).

## Structured allergy and problem lists unlock the strongest safety evidence

Medication decision support layered on electronic ordering has the best harm-reduction evidence of any EHR feature. Electronic ordering cut medication errors roughly in half compared with paper (RR 0.46) ([Nuckols et al. 2014](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/2046-4053-3-56)). In ICUs, commercial ordering with decision support was associated with **85% fewer prescribing errors and 12% lower mortality** ([Prgomet et al., JAMIA 2017](https://academic.oup.com/jamia/article/24/2/413/2907924)). AHRQ notes that without decision support, electronic ordering mostly prevents errors that would not have mattered clinically ([AHRQ](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK600580/)). CareVault already has the most useful starting point: prescriptions are written by generic, so it can match allergies at ingredient and class level and detect duplicate therapy without a commercial drug compendium. What it lacks is the patient data those rules need to check against: a coded allergy list that distinguishes "No Known Drug Allergies" from "not assessed" and allergy from intolerance, a problem list, and a recent weight.

The design constraint is alert fatigue. A 2024 meta-analysis found physicians override about **90%** of drug-interaction alerts ([Felisberto et al. 2024](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/14604582241263242)). Override rates rise with alert volume, reaching 98.6% for clinicians who see more than 5 alerts a day ([PubMed 41726460](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41726460/)). In one hospital, 88% of free-text override reasons were meaningless characters ([PubMed 38658357](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38658357)). CareVault should therefore start with a small set of high-severity rules in tiers: hard stop, interruptive, and passive. Overrides should require a reason chosen from a list. Every time an alert fires, is accepted or is overridden, the event should be logged so override rates can be reviewed per rule, and each organisation should be able to tune its rules. Opaque machine-learning prediction should stay out. On external validation, the Epic Sepsis Model achieved an AUC of 0.63 and a PPV of 12%, missed 67% of sepsis cases, and alerted on 18% of inpatients ([Wong et al., JAMA IM 2021](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2781307)). Rule-based screening built on NEWS2 plus suspected infection is transparent and works offline.

No affordable interaction database is built for Nigeria. Commercial compendia cost money. A pharmacist-curated table of the roughly 100–200 severe interactions relevant to the Nigerian essential medicines list is more realistic; examples include artemether-lumefantrine with QT-prolonging drugs and rifampicin with antiretrovirals. Most of the effort in this work is maintaining that content, not writing software.

## Inpatient workflows from eMAR to the Labour Care Guide are mostly checklists

CareVault's ADT module tracks where patients are but does not document what happens to them. The highest-evidence addition is the eMAR. In a study of 14,041 administrations, barcode eMAR cut non-timing administration errors by **41%** and potential adverse drug events by **51%**. Most of the transcription-error elimination came from replacing manual copying onto paper MARs ([Poon et al., NEJM 2010](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMsa0907115)). An eMAR without barcodes therefore captures much of the benefit on its own. Barcode scanning should come later, because many Nigerian products lack GS1 barcodes and hospitals would need to print their own labels first.

Electronic vitals with an automatically calculated early-warning score were associated with sustained mortality reductions at two UK hospitals ([Schmidt et al., BMJ Qual Saf 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25249636/)). NEWS2 predicted 7-day death in ED patients with a c-statistic of 0.865 ([PLOS One 2025](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0326058)). NEWS2, PEWS and MEOWS are simple arithmetic over vitals, so they need no ML and run offline.

The remaining workflow features are forms and checklists, so they are cheap to build. Several have strong evidence behind them. The I-PASS handoff bundle cut medical errors by **23%** and preventable adverse events by **30%** across nine hospitals ([Starmer et al., NEJM 2014](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMsa1405556)). In the original WHO Surgical Safety Checklist study, deaths fell from **1.5% to 0.8%** ([Haynes et al., NEJM 2009](https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/925e19ef-d9b6-4d88-b933-21583f35eb2a)), although a later Ontario population study is commonly read as showing no significant effect ([Urbach et al., NEJM 2014](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMsa1308261)). Among triage tools validated in low- and middle-income countries, the South African Triage Scale has the highest-quality evidence, with specificity of 91–97% ([PMC10026449](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10026449/)). For maternity, WHO's Labour Care Guide replaced the partograph in 2020 ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news/item/15-12-2020-monitoring-childbirth-in-a-new-era-for-maternal-health)), and Nigeria's maternal health burden makes it a priority module. No sourced mortality figure was gathered for this report.

## Nigeria Core gives interoperability a concrete R4 target

Until 2026, "be interoperable" in Nigeria meant little beyond DHIS2. Most respondents in a 10-state assessment knew of no other framework ([medRxiv 2025](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.27.25330401v1.full)). That has changed. The Nigeria Digital in Health Initiative publishes **Nigeria Core**, a FHIR **R4 (4.0.1)** implementation guide. It is a draft (version 0.0.0, dated 2026-06-01) and describes itself as the national interoperability "floor" for EMRs, registries, labs, pharmacy and claims, with a Shared Health Record aligned to the International Patient Summary ([Nigeria Core repo](https://github.com/digitalhealth-gov-ng/Nigeria-Core); [index](https://github.com/digitalhealth-gov-ng/Nigeria-Core/blob/main/input/pagecontent/index.md)). It profiles Patient, Organization, Encounter, Condition, Observation, MedicationRequest/Dispense, Immunization, Coverage, Claim, Consent and Provenance, and defines bundles for claim billing, pre-authorization, ePharmacy, referral and IPS ([profiles](https://github.com/digitalhealth-gov-ng/Nigeria-Core/tree/main/input/fsh/profiles)). Its draft security page requires OAuth2 client credentials or SMART tokens ([security.md](https://github.com/digitalhealth-gov-ng/Nigeria-Core/blob/main/input/pagecontent/security.md)). A pending Digital Health Services Bill would require FMoH licensing and compatibility with national systems. It had a first reading in March 2025, and its later status is unconfirmed ([Techhive](https://www.techhiveadvisory.africa/insights/review-of-nigerias-digital-health-services-bill-2025)).

The cheapest wins are data-model changes. NgPatient slices identifiers into NIN, MRN, birth certificate, mobile, insurance number and pseudonym, and codes the address to state, LGA and ward ([patient.fsh](https://github.com/digitalhealth-gov-ng/Nigeria-Core/blob/main/input/fsh/profiles/patient.fsh)). NgOrganization expects a Health Facility Registry identifier ([organization.fsh](https://github.com/digitalhealth-gov-ng/Nigeria-Core/blob/main/input/fsh/profiles/organization.fsh)), and the HFR list has been harmonised with the DHIS2 facility list ([MEASURE Evaluation](https://www.measureevaluation.org/resources/publications/tr-19-352.html)). Reporting is the next clearest value to customers. Facilities report monthly into DHIS2 using the NHMIS Monthly Summary Form, which is often tallied by hand from paper ([Frontiers 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/digital-health/articles/10.3389/fdgth.2022.985337/pdf)). NCDC uses SORMAS for IDSR, and electronic reporting improved timeliness from 43% to 73% compared with paper ([JIEPH 2026](https://afenet-journal.org/10-37432-jieph-d-26-00214/)). NIN coverage has gaps, especially among children, so CareVault still needs a local master patient index with fuzzy duplicate matching and a merge that can be reversed. OpenCR's combination of deterministic and probabilistic rules with a human review queue is a good model ([OpenCR](https://intrahealth.github.io/client-registry/user/recordlinkage/)).

Two cautions apply. SNOMED CT needs an affiliate licence in Nigeria, which is not a member country, and the free low-income tier probably does not apply ([SNOMED](https://www.snomed.org/get-snomed)). CareVault should therefore lean on ICD-11 and local value sets. Second, about half of Nigeria Core's planned guidance pages, including terminology and laboratory, are still empty, so CareVault should pin a version and follow the monthly releases.

## Operations, engagement, analytics and compliance round out the backlog

### FEFO stock and controlled-drug registers are expected locally

Nigerian pharmacy products already advertise batch-level stock, first-expiry-first-out (FEFO) allocation, one-step recalls and a NAFDAC controlled-substance register ([EziPharmaPOS](https://ezipharmapos.zarahcl.com/); [MediRxTrack](https://medirxtrack.com/)). MSH guidance specifies a two-signature controlled-drug register with a physical count after each entry ([MSH MDS-3](https://msh.org/wp-content/uploads/2013/04/mds3-ch46-facilitiespharmacymgmt-mar2012.pdf)). CareVault's dispense rows already store `batchNumber`, so tracing a recalled batch to the patients who received it needs little new work. Bed turnaround tooling has measurable effects: one bed-management system cut turnover time from 111 to 49 minutes ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/233900692_Improving_Bed_Turnover_Time_With_a_Bed_Management_System)).

### SMS reminders have Nigerian trial evidence; portal bill-pay reuses wallets

A Nigerian randomised trial found SMS reminders **halved** the risk of a missed appointment ([PubMed 27582239](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27582239/)). SMS also reaches basic handsets that WhatsApp cannot. Targeting phone calls at the 25% of patients at highest no-show risk cut no-shows from 19.3% to 15.9% ([PMC10624443](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10624443/)). MyChart's core features are proxy access, itemised bill-pay with payment plans, messaging, refills and questionnaires ([IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/software/radiology-workflow-informatics/patient-communication-portals/epic-mychart)). These are a sensible template, and CareVault's wallets make bill-pay cheap to add.

### GAID turns compliance paperwork into product features

GAID 2025 took effect on **19 September 2025**. Health-data processors count as being of major importance, which means DPIA, record-of-processing and annual Compliance Audit Return duties starting with the 2026 cycle. Higher-tier entities must file through a licensed DPCO ([DLA Piper](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2025/06/nigeria-ndpc-issues-gaid-key-compliance-insights/); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/data-protection/1781892/data-dignity-digital-health-balancing-technological-innovation-and-patient-privacy)). The NDPA requires breach notification to the NDPC within **72 hours** ([PrivacyNeedle](https://privacyneedle.com/compliance/legislation/the-72-hour-rule-what-ndpa-and-gdpr-say-about-data-breaches/)). Break-the-glass access needs three parts: the real-time override, automatic logging and mandatory review ([Censinet](https://censinet.com/perspectives/break-glass-access-pros-and-cons-for-healthcare)). The proposed HIPAA Security Rule update would make MFA mandatory ([Bradley](https://www.bradley.com/insights/publications/2025/03/top-10-takeaways-from-the-new-hipaa-security-rule-nprm)). That rule does not bind Nigerian hospitals, and the GAID summaries reviewed do not specify MFA. In line with the owner's decision, MFA stays out of the defaults. At most it could be an optional per-tenant setting, for example for platform admins, if a customer asks.

|---|---|---|---|

### Low-resource constraints shape how, not whether

These constraints apply to the whole roadmap. Unreliable power appears in about 60% of sub-Saharan EHR studies and poor connectivity in over 70% ([Mugauri et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12042231/)). OpenMRS O3 handles this with a facility LAN server that syncs to the cloud, and Bahmni with an offline PWA that syncs both ways ([O3 FAQ](https://openmrs.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/docs/pages/152731650/O3+Internet+Connection+FAQs); [Bahmni Connect](https://bahmni.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/BAH/pages/39813212/Bahmni+Connect+Features)). For CareVault, a P1 PWA with an offline read cache and a queued write outbox should cover vitals, eMAR, triage, registration and dispensing (L effort). Decision-support rule tables should be cached on the client. Print views and blank downtime forms (MAR, flowsheet, Labour Care Guide, receipts) should be P0-level polish on each module as it ships. Ghana's LHIMS was switched off in May 2025 over unpaid fees and then became embroiled in disputes over data control ([Techlabari](https://techlabari.com/lightwave-revoked-then-reactivated-its-digital-electronic-medical-record-services-for-ghana-health-institutions/)). That case makes bulk export and documented in-country hosting selling points, not afterthoughts.

## Conclusion

The research changes what "complete" means for a Nigerian EHR. The clinical modules CareVault already has are necessary, but they do not set it apart. In 2026, what makes an EHR worth buying is whether it can turn care into collected insurance revenue, and whether it can show regulators, under NHIA, GAID and soon Nigeria Core, an auditable trail of what happened. Both of those outcomes, as well as the safety features, depend on the same few structured foundations: a per-encounter charge ledger, coded allergies and problems, timestamped vitals and administrations, and well-identified patients and facilities. Building those first means each later feature mostly reuses existing data, from HMO claim batches to FHIR export, NHMIS reports and drug-interaction alerts.

Several assumptions are open and need checking before committing engineering time. The NHIA one-hour authorization rule and claim formats come from secondary sources, and the primary circulars should be obtained. Nigeria Core is a draft at version 0.0.0 and will change. No independent drug-interaction knowledge base is designed for Nigeria, so a pharmacist must own that content. Most safety evidence comes from US and UK hospitals in 2009–2017, so CareVault should measure its own alert override rates and early-warning-score escalations from the first release rather than assume the published effect sizes carry over.
